Finding

The open data has a hole in it.

The largest open list of Indian medicines (253,973 products, MIT-licensed, the usual source for RAG health bots) stores only the first two ingredients of every product. Cross-checking against full compositions shows that a quarter of its two-ingredient listings are really three or more. The missing one is often the paracetamol in a cold remedy.

khurak repairs what it can from full sources and flags the rest instead of silently trusting them.

Why a dictionary isn't enough

With 135,000 brand names, almost every word is a medicine somewhere: Cold, Rest, Pan, Life, even And (an ondansetron injection). of ordinary English word tokens match an Indian brand. khurak's small trained context model reads the surrounding words and decides whether a match is really a medicine.

Under the hood

How khurak checks a message

  1. Find candidates in English, Hinglish or Devanagari: exact brand, typo, phonetic match (कॉम्बिफ्लेम → Combiflam), or glued to a strength (dolo650).
  2. Read the context. A 100k-parameter char-CNN + BiGRU decides, from ±10 surrounding words, whether the match is a medicine.
  3. Resolve the variant. “Sinarest New tablet” vs “Sinarest New nasal spray”; “Dolo 650” vs “Dolo 250 syrup”.
  4. Check safety: same active ingredient twice, two drugs of one class (two NSAIDs), a short list of well-established dangerous combinations, daily totals above adult maxima, India's nimesulide ban for children, discontinued or incomplete data.
